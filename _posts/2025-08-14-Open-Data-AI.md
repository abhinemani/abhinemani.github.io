Recently I've seen an uptick in the [number](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/growing-relevance-open-data-generative-ai-marco-vernocchi-vtd8f%3FtrackingId=WMp8vaXnQoWPjGDfem8oKg%253D%253D/?trackingId=XCvDUGr4SQy%2FSm%2BPT%2FPlPA%3D%3D) of [civic](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/harnessing-power-generative-ai-world-open-government-data-zaidul-alam%3FtrackingId=CSGQGp2cQFeRkylweY1w%252FA%253D%253D/?trackingId=v6QQnlDbS1aBG6pHnX9I%2Fw%3D%3D) and [government](https://www.commerce.gov/news/blog/2024/01/preparing-open-data-age-ai) [tech](https://technical.ly/professional-development/data-analysis-llms-ai-guest-post/) [articles](https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/tech/technology/experts-urge-govt-to-invest-in-open-data-compute-for-ai-leadership/articleshow/107782457.cms?from=mdr) exploring how AI might breathe new life into the open data "movement" — there was [real, palpable momentum](https://beyondtransparency.org/) around open data in the last decade. I was closely involved in that [open data era](https://abhinemani.com/essays/2015/12/28/Putting-Open-Data-Work/) — with [wins](https://abhinemani.com/essays/2021/02/01/Let-s-Move-Beyond-Open-Data-Portals/) and [losses](https://abhinemani.com/blog/essays/2020/08/28/Open-Data-Civic-Engagement-and-Delivery/) — and wanted to reflect on what has changed and why it (maybe) matters. 

The resurgence of my enthusiasm for Open Data, catalyzed by the advancements in Artificial Intelligence (AI) and the widespread adoption of Software as a Service (SaaS) within local governments, comes with a tempered perspective. Having served as the first Chief Data Officer for the City of Los Angeles, I'm well versed in [previous open data hyperbole](https://abhinemani.com/academy), attuned to the waves of renewed optimism, and excited about the opportunity for all communities (no matter the size) to leverage open data and AI — thanks to the fundamental advantages of purpose-built government SaaS.

### Benefits of SaaS for Local Governments

* **Scalability & Accessibility:** Tailored to fit, SaaS grows with the community it serves, ensuring every town and city has access to cutting-edge tools.

* **Economic Prudence:** SaaS transforms financial outlays, replacing hefty upfront costs with manageable, ongoing expenses.

* **Innovation at the Fore:** Continuous updates keep local governments at the technological vanguard, eliminating the lag in adopting new features or critical security measures.

* **Collaborative Virtue:** By fostering a network where success stories and strategies are shared, SaaS amplifies the collective intelligence of civic entities.

### How GovTech SaaS Can Enable Better Open Data and Better AI

The evolution of SaaS solutions for local governments is particularly thrilling. These platforms are not just facilitating the digital transformation of municipal operations; they are creating a fertile ground for Open Data to thrive and be utilized in more meaningful ways. By standardizing processes, enhancing data collection, and improving data accessibility, SaaS is setting the stage for a future where Open Data isn’t just available but is integral to the decision-making process, policy formulation, and service delivery.

* **Renewed Optimism with SaaS and AI**: The integration of SaaS and AI into local governance rekindles my excitement for Open Data. SaaS platforms, by simplifying the management and accessibility of data, coupled with AI's ability to process and interpret this data, present a new horizon for Open Data's potential. For SaaS companies with large reach, the opportunity to prepare standardized, structured data for r[etrieval augmented generative AI (RAG)](https://www.govloop.com/community/blog/demystifying-generative-ai-myths-why-it-all-comes-down-to-what-you-feed-it/) is undeniable. And what's particularly exciting is that with SaaS, not every city has to invest in its own LLM: AI models built off SaaS platforms scale by definition. This shift towards a more interconnected and accessible data ecosystem is pivotal for realizing Open Data's potential through AI.

* **Skepticism as a Lens for Progress**: My optimism is now tempered with a healthy skepticism, shaped by past experiences. It's crucial to acknowledge that initial high expectations for Open Data led to some disappointment. Despite the promise of transparency and engagement, the practical utilization of Open Data often fell short, primarily due to technological and organizational barriers. This past skepticism isn't a deterrent but a catalyst for a more realistic and focused approach to harnessing Open Data. It drives a commitment to overcoming the barriers that previously hindered Open Data's success.

* **New Opportunities for Open Data & Personalized Insight**: AI and LLMs offer a path to transcend previous limitations by turning complex datasets into actionable insights. Their role in analyzing and leveraging Open Data is crucial for informed decision-making and enhancing public engagement. AI can analyze Open Data to understand patterns and preferences at an individual and community level, enabling the delivery of tailored public services. Critically open data can now be more personalized. Instead of visiting spreadsheets on the internet for "transparency," GenAI can produce hyper-relevant content for residents, fostering a more informed and engaged citizenry. Residents can have better insights into how their cities operate, the challenges they face, and the impact of policy decisions, encouraging a more active participation in civic life.

As we navigate the renewed journey of Open Data, infused with the capabilities of SaaS and AI, it's essential to carry forward the lessons of the past. This approach doesn't dampen our enthusiasm but grounds it in a realism that seeks to avoid previous pitfalls while unlocking new possibilities for public service. This balanced perspective not only acknowledges the complexity of transforming government operations but also celebrates the potential for meaningful change.
